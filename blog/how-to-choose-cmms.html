Choosing a CMMS is one of the most consequential decisions a maintenance team makes. The right system transforms how your team works. The wrong one becomes expensive shelfware that nobody uses.

This guide walks you through the key criteria for evaluating CMMS software, the most common mistakes organisations make during selection, and a practical checklist you can use to compare your options.

Before You Start: Define What You Need

Before you look at any software, take time to document your requirements. This sounds obvious, but many organisations skip this step and end up choosing a system based on marketing demos rather than actual needs.

Questions to Answer First

  • How many assets do you need to manage?
  • How many users will need access (technicians, managers, operators)?
  • How many sites or locations are involved?
  • What are you using now (spreadsheets, paper, another CMMS)?
  • What are your biggest maintenance pain points?
  • Do you have specific compliance or regulatory requirements?
  • What is your budget — both initial and ongoing?
  • Do technicians need mobile access in the field?

Write these down. Share them with your team. Use them as the foundation for every evaluation conversation. A vendor who cannot clearly address your specific needs is not the right fit.

1. Ease of Use

This is the single most important factor. A CMMS that is difficult to use will not be used — no matter how many features it has. Your maintenance technicians are skilled tradespeople, not software specialists. The system must be intuitive enough that they can learn it quickly and use it willingly.

When evaluating ease of use, do not rely on the vendor's demo alone. Ask for a trial account and have your actual technicians try it. Watch how they interact with the system without guidance.

  • Clean, uncluttered interface that is not overwhelming
  • Logical navigation that matches maintenance workflows
  • Minimal clicks to complete common tasks (create work order, update status)
  • Consistent design patterns throughout the application
  • No requirement for technical expertise or extensive training

If a system requires weeks of training before your team can use it effectively, that is a warning sign. The best CMMS platforms feel familiar from the first login.

2. Implementation Time

Some CMMS implementations take 6-12 months. Others take days. The difference usually comes down to the complexity of the system and the vendor's approach.

Long implementations are costly — not just in consultant fees, but in delayed value. Every month you spend implementing is a month your team is still using spreadsheets.

  • Can you import your asset list quickly (CSV upload, templates)?
  • Is the system pre-configured or does everything need custom setup?
  • Does the vendor provide onboarding support?
  • Can you start using core features (work orders, assets) immediately?
  • Is there a phased implementation path that delivers early value?

Ask vendors directly: "How long until my team is using this daily?" If the answer involves months of configuration, workshops, and customisation — consider whether that complexity is truly necessary for your needs.

3. Mobile Experience

Maintenance happens in the field. If the CMMS does not work well on a mobile device, your technicians will not use it where it matters most. Mobile is not a nice-to-have — it is essential.

Be specific when evaluating mobile. There is a significant difference between a desktop interface squeezed onto a phone screen and a purpose-built mobile experience.

  • Responsive design that works on phones and tablets
  • Core tasks (view, update, close work orders) fully functional on mobile
  • Camera integration for capturing photos directly into work orders
  • QR code or barcode scanning for asset identification
  • Push notifications for new and urgent tasks
  • Usable with work gloves and in outdoor lighting conditions

Test the mobile experience yourself. Open the CMMS on your phone, navigate to a work order, and try to update it. If it is frustrating for you, it will be worse for a technician doing it thirty times a day.

4. Customer Support

Every CMMS vendor claims to offer great support. The reality varies enormously. Some provide dedicated support teams with fast response times. Others route you through chatbots and ticket queues that take days to resolve.

Support matters most during implementation and the first few months of use. Your team will have questions, encounter unexpected situations, and need guidance. Responsive support during this period is critical to successful adoption.

  • What support channels are available (phone, email, chat)?
  • What are the response time guarantees?
  • Is support included in the price or charged separately?
  • Is onboarding assistance provided?
  • Is there a knowledge base, help centre, or training videos?
  • Do you get a dedicated account contact or go through a generic queue?

Ask for references from existing customers and specifically ask about their support experience. The sales process is not representative of the ongoing relationship.

5. Total Cost of Ownership

CMMS pricing can be deceptively simple on the surface and surprisingly complex underneath. A low per-user price means little if implementation, training, integrations, and support are all extra.

Understand the full cost before you commit. Ask vendors to provide a total cost estimate for your first year and your third year — including everything.

  • Monthly or annual subscription fees
  • Per-user pricing vs. flat-rate pricing
  • Implementation and setup fees
  • Training costs
  • Data migration costs
  • Integration fees for connecting to other systems
  • Premium support or dedicated account management charges
  • Cost of adding additional users or sites later

Also consider the cost of not using a CMMS — continued downtime, reactive repair costs, compliance risk, and the productivity your team is losing right now. The cheapest option is rarely the best value.

6. Feature Fit

More features do not always mean a better system. In fact, overly complex CMMS platforms with hundreds of features often suffer from poor usability and low adoption. The goal is not to find the system with the most features — it is to find the one whose features match your needs.

Essential Features for Most Teams

  • Work order management (create, assign, track, close)
  • Asset register with maintenance history
  • Preventive maintenance scheduling
  • Mobile access for field technicians
  • Reporting and dashboards
  • Spare parts and inventory tracking

Valuable but Not Always Essential

  • Maintenance request portal for non-maintenance staff
  • Purchase order management
  • Contractor and vendor management
  • IoT and sensor integration
  • Multi-site management with cross-site reporting
  • API access for custom integrations

Be wary of vendors who try to sell you features you do not need. A simpler system that your team uses daily will outperform a feature-rich system that gathers dust.

7. Scalability

Choose a CMMS that can grow with you. Even if you are starting with one site and a small team, consider what your needs might look like in two or three years.

  • Can you add new sites without a major reconfiguration?
  • Is there a cost-effective way to add more users?
  • Does the system handle large asset registers without performance issues?
  • Can you set up different permissions for different teams or sites?
  • Is the vendor actively developing and improving the platform?

A CMMS is a long-term investment. Switching systems is disruptive and expensive. Choose one that will not limit you as your organisation evolves.

Common Mistakes When Choosing a CMMS

After seeing hundreds of CMMS selection processes, these are the mistakes that cause the most problems.

Choosing Based on Features Alone

A feature comparison spreadsheet can be misleading. The system with the most ticks is not necessarily the best fit. Usability, support quality, and implementation speed matter just as much — often more.

Not Involving Technicians in the Decision

The people who will use the CMMS daily should have a voice in the selection. If technicians find the system difficult or impractical, adoption will fail regardless of what management decided.

Underestimating Implementation Effort

Some organisations buy a CMMS expecting it to transform their operations overnight. Effective implementation takes planning, data preparation, configuration, training, and ongoing refinement. Budget time for it.

Ignoring Mobile Requirements

Evaluating the CMMS only on a desktop computer misses the most important use case. Your technicians are in the field. If the mobile experience is poor, the system will not be used where it counts.

Falling for the Demo Effect

Vendor demos are carefully choreographed. The system always looks clean, fast, and easy during a demo. Insist on a hands-on trial where your team uses the system with your own data and real workflows.

Choosing the Cheapest Option

Price is important, but the cheapest CMMS often comes with hidden costs — poor support, limited features, difficult migrations, or a clunky interface that kills adoption. Evaluate total value, not just the subscription price.

Buying More Than You Need

Enterprise-grade CMMS platforms with hundreds of modules sound impressive but can be overkill for teams that need solid work orders, asset tracking, and preventive maintenance. Match the system to your reality, not your aspirations.

CMMS Evaluation Checklist

Use this checklist when comparing CMMS vendors. Score each item and compare totals across your shortlisted options.

Usability

  • Interface is clean and intuitive
  • Common tasks require minimal clicks
  • Technicians can learn the system quickly
  • Navigation is logical and consistent
  • Your team tested it (not just watched a demo)

Core Features

  • Work order creation, assignment, and tracking
  • Asset register with full maintenance history
  • Preventive maintenance scheduling (time, meter, condition)
  • Spare parts and inventory management
  • Reporting dashboards and KPIs
  • Maintenance request submission for non-technical users

Mobile

  • Full functionality on mobile devices
  • Photo capture from within work orders
  • QR/barcode scanning for assets
  • Push notifications for task assignments
  • Usable in real field conditions

Implementation

  • Clear onboarding process with timeline
  • Easy data import (CSV, templates)
  • Guided setup or onboarding assistance
  • Can start using core features within days, not months
  • Phased implementation approach available

Support

  • Responsive support with clear SLAs
  • Multiple support channels (phone, email, chat)
  • Support included in the subscription price
  • Knowledge base and training resources
  • Positive references from existing customers

Pricing

  • Transparent pricing with no hidden costs
  • Reasonable cost to add users and sites
  • No separate charges for core features
  • Free trial available to test before committing
  • Flexible contract terms (monthly or annual)

Scalability and Future-Proofing

  • Multi-site support
  • Role-based access control
  • Active product development and regular updates
  • API available for integrations
  • Data export so you are not locked in

Key Questions to Ask CMMS Vendors

When you are speaking with CMMS vendors, these questions cut through the marketing and reveal what the experience will actually be like.

About the Product

  • Can we try the system with our own data before committing?
  • How often do you release product updates?
  • What does the mobile experience look like for technicians?
  • How do you handle data backup and security?
  • Can we export our data if we decide to leave?

About Implementation

  • What is the typical time from sign-up to daily use?
  • What data import formats do you support?
  • Do you provide onboarding support or is it self-service?
  • What does a typical implementation plan look like?

About Support and Pricing

  • What is included in the subscription and what costs extra?
  • What are your support response times?
  • Can we speak to existing customers in our industry?
  • What happens to our data and access if we cancel?
  • Are there volume discounts for adding more users or sites?

A confident vendor will answer these questions directly. Evasive or vague answers are a red flag.

Red Flags to Watch For

During your evaluation, be alert to these warning signs that may indicate a poor fit.

  • No free trial available — vendors confident in their product let you try it
  • Implementation takes months — unless your requirements are truly complex, this suggests an overcomplicated system
  • Pricing requires a custom quote for basic information — transparency matters
  • Support is an add-on — support should be included, especially during onboarding
  • Mobile is an afterthought — if the mobile app feels like a stripped-down version, it will not work in the field
  • No data export option — you should always be able to take your data with you
  • Pushy sales tactics — high-pressure closing suggests the product does not sell itself
  • No customer references — every established vendor should have customers willing to share their experience

A Practical Evaluation Process

Here is a simple process to follow when selecting a CMMS. It keeps things structured without turning the selection into a six-month project.

Step 1: Document Your Requirements

Spend a day writing down what you need. Talk to your technicians, your manager, and anyone else who will be affected. Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves.

Step 2: Create a Shortlist

Research 4-6 CMMS platforms that appear to match your needs. Eliminate options that clearly do not fit (wrong industry, wrong scale, wrong price range).

Step 3: Request Demos

Ask each vendor for a demo focused on your specific use cases — not a generic walkthrough. Bring your requirements list and ask them to show how the system handles each one.

Step 4: Run a Hands-On Trial

Narrow to 2-3 finalists and request trial accounts. Have your actual users — especially technicians — test the system with real scenarios. This is the most important step and the one most organisations skip.

Step 5: Check References

Ask each finalist for customer references, preferably in a similar industry or team size. Ask about implementation, support, and whether the system delivered on its promises.

Step 6: Compare and Decide

Use your evaluation checklist to score each finalist. Weight the criteria based on what matters most to your organisation. Make the decision as a team, not in isolation.

Summary

Choosing the right CMMS comes down to finding a system that your team will use, that solves your specific problems, and that is backed by a vendor who will support you beyond the sale. Features matter, but usability, implementation speed, mobile experience, and support quality matter more.

Take the time to define your needs, involve your team in the evaluation, and insist on a hands-on trial before committing. The effort you invest in choosing well will pay dividends for years.

The best CMMS is not the one with the longest feature list. It is the one your maintenance team actually uses — every day, on every job.
