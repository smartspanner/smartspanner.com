Introduction

Choosing the right CMMS (Computerised Maintenance Management System) is a critical decision for any organisation managing equipment and maintenance operations.

The right system can improve efficiency, reduce downtime, and streamline workflows — while the wrong choice can lead to poor adoption and wasted investment.

This guide explains how to choose a CMMS that fits your business needs.

Why Choosing the Right CMMS Matters

A CMMS becomes central to your maintenance operations.

If it is:

  • Too complex — teams won't use it
  • Too basic — it won't scale
  • Poorly implemented — it won't deliver value

Choosing correctly ensures long-term success.

Key Factors to Consider

1. Ease of Use

The system must be simple and intuitive.

If technicians find it difficult:

  • Adoption will drop
  • Data will be incomplete
  • Processes will fail

Look for a system that your team can start using after a short demo — not weeks of training.

2. Implementation Speed

Look for a system that can be deployed quickly.

Avoid:

  • Long setup times
  • Heavy configuration requirements
  • Complex onboarding processes

The faster your team is up and running, the faster you see value.

3. Core Features

Ensure the CMMS includes the functionality your team needs:

  • Work order management
  • Preventive maintenance scheduling
  • Asset tracking
  • Reporting and analytics
  • Spare parts management

Focus on what you need now — not features you may never use.

4. Mobile Access

Maintenance happens on the shop floor — not at a desk.

A good CMMS should:

  • Work on mobile devices and tablets
  • Be easy for technicians to use in the field
  • Allow real-time updates from anywhere

If technicians can't use it on the shop floor, adoption will be limited.

5. Scalability

Your system should grow with your business.

Consider:

  • Number of users
  • Number of assets and sites
  • Future complexity and requirements

Choose a system that can handle your current needs and scale as your operations grow.

6. Support and Onboarding

Good support is essential — especially during the first few weeks.

Look for:

  • Fast response times
  • Training resources and documentation
  • Ongoing assistance without extra cost

Avoid systems where support is locked behind premium plans.

7. Total Cost of Ownership

Look beyond the monthly price. Consider:

  • Setup and onboarding fees
  • Per-user pricing at scale
  • Cost of additional features or support
  • Training and implementation costs

The cheapest system is not always the best value.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

When selecting a CMMS, avoid these common pitfalls:

  • Choosing based on features alone — more features doesn't mean better fit
  • Ignoring usability — if your team won't use it, features don't matter
  • Underestimating implementation effort — complex systems take longer to deliver value
  • Not involving end users — technicians should be part of the evaluation
  • Skipping the trial — always test the system with your actual workflows

CMMS Evaluation Checklist

Use this checklist when comparing systems:

Criteria What to Look For
Ease of Use Intuitive interface, minimal training needed
Implementation Quick setup, no complex configuration
Mobile Full functionality on phones and tablets
Core Features Work orders, PM, assets, reporting
Support Responsive, included in pricing
Scalability Grows with users, assets, and sites
Cost Transparent pricing, no hidden fees

Conclusion

The best CMMS is not the most complex — it is the one your team will actually use.

Focus on usability, speed, and practical functionality to ensure long-term success. A system that your team adopts quickly will deliver far more value than one packed with features nobody uses.
