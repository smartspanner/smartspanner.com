Introduction

Predictive maintenance is an advanced maintenance strategy that uses real-time data and analytics to predict when equipment is likely to fail.

Instead of reacting to breakdowns or relying on fixed schedules, predictive maintenance allows organisations to act at the right time — reducing downtime, improving efficiency, and lowering costs.

In this guide, we explain what predictive maintenance is, how it works, and how it compares to other maintenance strategies.

What is Predictive Maintenance?

Predictive maintenance is a data-driven approach to maintenance that uses monitoring, sensors, and analytics to identify potential equipment failures before they happen.

The goal is simple: perform maintenance only when it is actually needed — not too early, and not too late.

How Predictive Maintenance Works

Predictive maintenance combines several components:

1. Data Collection

Sensors and devices monitor equipment in real time, capturing data such as:

  • Vibration
  • Temperature
  • Pressure
  • Usage and power consumption

2. Data Analysis

Software analyses this data to detect patterns, anomalies, and signs of wear or failure. Over time, the system builds a clearer picture of normal equipment behaviour — making it easier to spot deviations.

3. Alerts and Insights

When an issue is detected or a threshold is exceeded, the system generates alerts so maintenance teams can take action before a failure occurs.

4. Maintenance Action

Work orders are created and tasks are scheduled at the optimal time — avoiding both unnecessary servicing and unexpected breakdowns.

Key Benefits of Predictive Maintenance

Reduce Unplanned Downtime

Identify problems early and avoid unexpected failures that disrupt operations.

Lower Maintenance Costs

Avoid unnecessary maintenance and reduce expensive emergency repairs.

Improve Asset Reliability

Maintain equipment based on actual condition, not assumptions.

Increase Operational Efficiency

Optimise maintenance schedules and resource allocation.

Extend Equipment Lifespan

Prevent damage caused by undetected issues developing over time.

Real-World Example

A manufacturing company monitors machine vibration using sensors.

Before predictive maintenance:

  • Machines fail unexpectedly
  • Downtime disrupts production
  • Repairs are reactive and expensive

After implementing predictive maintenance:

  • Early warning signs are detected automatically
  • Maintenance is scheduled in advance
  • Downtime is reduced significantly

Challenges of Predictive Maintenance

While powerful, predictive maintenance can be difficult to implement with separate tools.

Common challenges include:

  • Selecting the right sensors for your equipment
  • Integrating sensor data with maintenance systems
  • Managing large volumes of data
  • Turning insights into maintenance actions

This is why an integrated solution — where monitoring and maintenance management are in one system — is significantly easier to implement.

Predictive Maintenance and IoT

Predictive maintenance is closely linked to IoT (Internet of Things).

IoT devices:

  • Collect real-time data continuously
  • Enable continuous monitoring without manual checks
  • Provide the foundation for predictive insights
  • Integrate with CMMS to automate maintenance actions

For a practical guide on monitoring technologies, see our article on IoT condition monitoring.

When Should You Use Predictive Maintenance?

Predictive maintenance is ideal when:

  • Equipment is critical to operations
  • Downtime is costly
  • Failures are difficult to predict manually
  • Data can be collected from equipment using sensors

Not every asset needs predictive maintenance. For a framework on which approach to use for different assets, see our guide on condition-based vs time-based maintenance.

Best Practices

To get the most from predictive maintenance:

  • Start with critical assets where failure is most costly
  • Use the right sensors for your equipment type
  • Define clear alert thresholds
  • Integrate monitoring with a CMMS system
  • Track maintenance KPIs to measure impact
  • Continuously refine your approach based on data

Conclusion

Predictive maintenance represents the next step in maintenance evolution.

By using data to predict failures, organisations can reduce downtime, lower costs, and improve performance.

As technology becomes more accessible, predictive maintenance is no longer just for large enterprises — it is becoming a practical option for modern maintenance teams of all sizes.
