Introduction

IoT condition monitoring uses connected sensors to track equipment health in real time — providing maintenance teams with continuous visibility into the condition of their assets.

Instead of relying on scheduled inspections or waiting for failures, condition monitoring detects problems as they develop — enabling faster, more targeted maintenance action.

This guide explains how IoT condition monitoring works, the types of monitoring available, and how to implement it effectively.

What is Condition Monitoring?

Condition monitoring is the process of tracking specific parameters of equipment to identify changes that indicate developing faults.

Traditional condition monitoring involved periodic manual checks. IoT-enabled condition monitoring does this continuously and automatically using connected sensors.

Key parameters monitored include:

  • Vibration
  • Temperature
  • Power consumption
  • Pressure and flow
  • Humidity and environmental conditions

How IoT Enables Condition Monitoring

IoT transforms condition monitoring from a manual, periodic activity into a continuous, automated process.

Continuous Data Collection

Sensors installed on equipment collect data 24/7 without manual intervention. This provides a far more complete picture than periodic inspections.

Wireless Connectivity

Modern IoT sensors transmit data wirelessly to cloud platforms, eliminating the need for complex wiring or manual data retrieval.

Real-Time Alerts

When sensor readings exceed defined thresholds, the system generates instant alerts — notifying maintenance teams before a failure occurs.

Integration with CMMS

Sensor data can flow directly into a CMMS, automatically triggering work orders and maintenance actions based on real equipment condition.

Types of Condition Monitoring

Vibration Monitoring

What it detects: Bearing wear, imbalance, misalignment, looseness, and mechanical degradation.

Best for: Rotating equipment — motors, pumps, compressors, fans, and spindles.

Vibration monitoring is one of the most widely used and effective forms of condition monitoring. Changes in vibration patterns are often the earliest indicator of developing faults.

Temperature Monitoring

What it detects: Overheating in motors, bearings, electrical panels, hydraulic systems, and cooling circuits.

Best for: Any equipment where heat is a sign of friction, overload, or failure.

Temperature sensors are inexpensive and easy to deploy, making them a good starting point for condition monitoring.

Power / Current Monitoring

What it detects: Motor degradation, efficiency losses, power quality issues, and abnormal load patterns.

Best for: Electric motors, drives, and power-intensive equipment.

Tracking power consumption patterns can reveal problems that are not visible through visual inspection.

Flow and Pressure Monitoring

What it detects: Blockages, leaks, pump degradation, and valve failures.

Best for: Hydraulic systems, pneumatic systems, cooling circuits, and fluid processing equipment.

Humidity and Environmental Monitoring

What it detects: Environmental conditions that can damage equipment, products, or materials.

Best for: Clean rooms, storage areas, server rooms, and sensitive production environments.

Benefits of IoT Condition Monitoring

  • Detect problems early — before they cause equipment failure
  • Reduce unplanned downtime — act on real data, not assumptions
  • Lower maintenance costs — maintain only when condition data shows it's needed
  • Extend asset lifespan — avoid both under-maintenance and over-maintenance
  • Improve safety — prevent catastrophic failures and hazardous conditions
  • Enable predictive maintenance — use data to predict and prevent failures

How to Implement IoT Condition Monitoring

Step 1: Identify Critical Assets

Focus on equipment where failure causes the most disruption, cost, or safety risk. Not every asset needs condition monitoring.

Step 2: Choose What to Monitor

Select the parameters that best indicate equipment health for each asset. Vibration and temperature are common starting points.

Step 3: Select Sensors

Choose sensors appropriate for your environment. Consider wireless connectivity, battery life, accuracy, and mounting options.

Step 4: Install and Configure

Install sensors on critical assets and configure thresholds for alerts. Modern IoT sensors can often be deployed in minutes.

Step 5: Integrate with Your CMMS

Connect sensor data to your maintenance system so that alerts automatically create work orders and inform maintenance scheduling.

Step 6: Monitor and Optimise

Review data regularly, refine thresholds based on experience, and expand monitoring to additional assets over time.

Condition Monitoring vs Scheduled Maintenance

Fixed intervals (time-based) Based on real equipment condition

Best Practices

  • Start with critical assets — don't try to monitor everything at once
  • Define clear thresholds — set alert levels based on manufacturer data and operational experience
  • Integrate with your CMMS — ensure alerts drive real maintenance actions
  • Review data regularly — use trends to refine your maintenance strategy
  • Combine with preventive maintenance — condition monitoring complements, not replaces, scheduled maintenance

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Monitoring too many assets too soon
  • Setting thresholds too high (missing early warnings) or too low (excessive alerts)
  • Collecting data without connecting it to maintenance actions
  • Using separate systems for monitoring and maintenance management

Conclusion

IoT condition monitoring gives maintenance teams real-time visibility into equipment health — enabling faster, more targeted maintenance and significantly reducing unplanned downtime.

The key is to start with critical assets, choose the right monitoring parameters, integrate with your CMMS, and build from there.

Combined with a structured preventive maintenance programme, condition monitoring delivers a powerful, data-driven approach to maintenance management.
