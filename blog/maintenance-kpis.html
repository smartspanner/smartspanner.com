Introduction

Maintenance KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) are essential for understanding how effectively your maintenance operations are performing.

Without clear metrics, it's difficult to:

  • Identify inefficiencies
  • Reduce downtime
  • Improve asset reliability

In this guide, we break down the most important maintenance KPIs, what they mean, and how to use them to improve performance.

What Are Maintenance KPIs?

Maintenance KPIs are measurable values used to evaluate the performance of maintenance activities.

They help organisations:

  • Track efficiency
  • Monitor equipment reliability
  • Improve decision-making
  • Justify investment in maintenance resources

Why Maintenance KPIs Matter

Tracking KPIs allows you to:

  • Identify recurring issues before they escalate
  • Reduce unplanned downtime
  • Improve maintenance planning
  • Optimise resource allocation

Without KPIs, maintenance becomes reactive and difficult to control.

Key Maintenance KPIs You Should Track

1. Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF)

Measures the average time between equipment failures.

Formula: Total operating time / Number of failures

Higher MTBF means more reliable equipment. Tracking this over time shows whether your preventive maintenance programme is improving reliability.

2. Mean Time to Repair (MTTR)

Measures how quickly equipment is repaired after failure.

Formula: Total repair time / Number of repairs

Lower MTTR means faster recovery and less downtime. If MTTR is increasing, it may indicate issues with parts availability, technician skills, or work order processes.

3. Equipment Downtime

Tracks how long equipment is unavailable due to maintenance or failure.

Reducing downtime directly improves productivity and output. Track downtime by asset to identify your worst performers.

4. Planned vs Unplanned Maintenance Ratio

Compares scheduled maintenance with reactive work.

Formula: Planned work orders / Total work orders x 100

Higher planned maintenance means better control. Best-in-class organisations target 80% planned / 20% reactive. For more on this, see our guide on reactive vs preventive maintenance.

5. Work Order Completion Rate

Measures how many maintenance tasks are completed on time.

Formula: Work orders completed / Work orders created x 100

Indicates team productivity and efficiency. A growing backlog of incomplete work orders signals capacity or prioritisation issues.

6. Maintenance Cost per Asset

Tracks how much is spent maintaining each asset.

Helps identify costly equipment that may need replacement, different maintenance strategies, or further investigation.

7. Preventive Maintenance Compliance

Measures how consistently preventive maintenance tasks are completed on time.

Formula: PM tasks completed on time / Total PM tasks scheduled x 100

High compliance reduces breakdowns. Target: 90% or higher.

How to Use Maintenance KPIs

Tracking KPIs is only valuable if you act on them.

Step 1: Define Clear Targets

Set realistic benchmarks for each KPI based on your industry and current performance.

Step 2: Monitor Regularly

Track performance consistently — weekly or monthly depending on your operation.

Step 3: Identify Trends

Look for patterns in failures, delays, or costs. Are certain assets consistently underperforming?

Step 4: Take Action

Adjust maintenance strategies based on insights. If MTBF is declining for an asset, review its maintenance plan.

Common Challenges

Many organisations struggle with KPI tracking because of:

  • Incomplete data — not all work is recorded
  • Manual tracking in spreadsheets — error-prone and time-consuming
  • Lack of visibility — no centralised system
  • Inconsistent processes — different teams recording things differently

These issues make KPIs unreliable — which is why using a CMMS is essential.

Real-World Example

A manufacturing company tracks MTBF and notices frequent failures on a specific machine.

By analysing the data:

  • They identify a recurring bearing issue
  • Adjust maintenance schedules to include more frequent inspections
  • Reduce breakdowns on that machine by addressing the root cause

Result: Improved reliability, reduced downtime, lower costs.

Best Practices for Maintenance KPIs

  • Focus on a core set of KPIs — start with 3-5, don't try to track everything
  • Ensure data accuracy — use a CMMS to automate collection
  • Review performance regularly — schedule monthly KPI reviews
  • Align KPIs with business goals — track what matters to your operation
  • Act on the data — KPIs are only useful if they drive decisions
