Introduction

Maintenance KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) help organisations measure and improve maintenance performance.

Without clear metrics, it is difficult to know whether maintenance is improving, where problems exist, or how resources are being used.

In this guide, we cover the most important maintenance KPIs, how to calculate them, and how to use them to drive better results.

Why KPIs Matter

Tracking the right KPIs provides:

  • Visibility — understand what is happening across your maintenance operations
  • Accountability — ensure tasks are completed and standards are met
  • Data-driven insights — make decisions based on evidence, not assumptions
  • Continuous improvement — identify trends and areas for optimisation

Without KPIs, maintenance management is based on guesswork.

Key Maintenance KPIs

1. Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF)

What it measures: Equipment reliability — the average time between breakdowns.

Formula: Total operating time / Number of failures

Why it matters: A higher MTBF indicates more reliable equipment. Tracking this helps you identify assets that need attention and measure the effectiveness of your preventive maintenance programme.

2. Mean Time to Repair (MTTR)

What it measures: Maintenance efficiency — how quickly repairs are completed.

Formula: Total repair time / Number of repairs

Why it matters: A lower MTTR means faster repairs and less downtime. If MTTR is increasing, it may indicate issues with parts availability, technician skills, or work order processes.

3. Preventive Maintenance Compliance

What it measures: How consistently scheduled maintenance is completed on time.

Formula: PM tasks completed on time / Total PM tasks scheduled x 100

Why it matters: Low PM compliance means scheduled maintenance is being missed — increasing the risk of breakdowns. Target: 90% or higher.

4. Equipment Downtime

What it measures: Total time equipment is unavailable due to maintenance or failure.

Why it matters: Downtime directly impacts production output and revenue. Tracking downtime by asset helps you prioritise asset management efforts.

5. Planned vs Unplanned Maintenance Ratio

What it measures: The balance between proactive and reactive work.

Formula: Planned work orders / Total work orders x 100

Why it matters: A higher ratio of planned work indicates a more proactive maintenance operation. Best-in-class organisations target 80% planned / 20% reactive. See our guide on reactive vs preventive maintenance.

6. Work Order Completion Rate

What it measures: Productivity — the percentage of work orders completed within a given period.

Formula: Work orders completed / Work orders created x 100

Why it matters: A growing backlog of incomplete work orders indicates capacity or prioritisation issues.

7. Maintenance Cost as % of Asset Value

What it measures: Whether you are spending the right amount on maintaining your assets.

Formula: Total maintenance cost / Total asset replacement value x 100

Why it matters: Industry benchmarks typically range from 2-5%. Spending too little may indicate under-maintenance; spending too much may suggest inefficiency.

8. Maintenance Backlog

What it measures: The volume of outstanding maintenance work.

Why it matters: A large or growing backlog means your team cannot keep up with demand. This can lead to missed maintenance and increased failure risk.

9. Mean Time to Acknowledge (MTTA)

What it measures: How quickly maintenance requests are acknowledged after being raised.

Why it matters: Slow response times can indicate communication issues or resource constraints. Faster acknowledgement leads to faster resolution.

10. Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE)

What it measures: A combined metric of equipment availability, performance, and quality.

Formula: Availability x Performance x Quality

Why it matters: OEE provides a holistic view of how effectively equipment is being used. It highlights losses from downtime, slow running, and defects.

How to Track KPIs Effectively

  • Use a CMMS — centralise data collection and reporting
  • Start with 3-5 KPIs — don't try to track everything at once
  • Review regularly — weekly or monthly depending on your operation
  • Set clear targets — define what good looks like for each metric
  • Act on the data — KPIs are only useful if they drive decisions

Conclusion

Tracking the right KPIs helps you understand and optimise your maintenance operations.

Start with the metrics that matter most to your business, use a CMMS to automate data collection, and review performance regularly to drive continuous improvement.
