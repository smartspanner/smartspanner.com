What is a CMMS?

A CMMS (Computerised Maintenance Management System) gives maintenance teams one operational record for the work they plan, the assets they maintain and the history they need afterwards.

Instead of keeping requests, schedules, photographs, parts and completion records in separate places, a CMMS connects them around the maintenance job. Teams use it to:

  • Schedule and track work orders
  • Manage preventive maintenance plans
  • Keep service, breakdown and document history against each asset
  • Record parts, labour, photographs and completion details
  • Retrieve maintenance evidence and review performance

Smartspanner is cloud based, with browser access for managers and connected native mobile apps for technicians. It was first built inside a manufacturing operation that needed a better maintenance record without a lengthy implementation project.

Maintenance Information Is Often Split Across the Operation

The maintenance process may already exist, but the record is fragmented. A request arrives by message, planned work sits in a spreadsheet, photographs stay on a phone and the useful detail from a breakdown remains with the person who attended it.

That creates practical gaps:

  • Nobody has one current view of what is due, late or waiting
  • Technicians must search for information before starting the job
  • The same finding is entered again when it becomes corrective work
  • Asset history is reconstructed after a failure or before an audit
  • Reporting becomes a separate administrative exercise

A useful CMMS closes those gaps while keeping the common workflow short enough to use at the point of work.

CMMS vs EAM — What's the Difference?

CMMS (Computerised Maintenance Management Systems) focus on the execution and record of maintenance: work orders, preventive maintenance, assets, parts and technician activity.

EAM (Enterprise Asset Management) usually extends further across financial, procurement and lifecycle governance for an organisation's wider asset portfolio. Product boundaries vary, so the label alone is not enough to choose between them.

A maintenance team should test whether the proposed system supports:

  • The complete job from request or schedule through completion
  • The asset, inventory, safety and reporting controls actually required
  • Technician adoption at the point of work
  • Data export, API access and integration with the wider operation

How to Choose the Right CMMS Software

When selecting a CMMS, consider:

  • Run one real request or planned job through the technician workflow
  • Confirm what works in the browser, native apps and available connectivity
  • Test asset import, data export and the API requirements you expect to use
  • Identify implementation work, internal ownership and available support
  • Compare the full subscription and service cost over the intended term
  • Ask technicians and managers to assess the same workflow from their own views

Frequently Asked Questions

What does CMMS stand for?

CMMS stands for Computerised Maintenance Management System. It is software used to manage maintenance operations, assets, work orders, and spare parts in a single platform.

Is CMMS suitable for small businesses?

Yes. A small team can begin with assets, work orders and preventive maintenance, then add procedures, inventory, inspections and other controls when they are useful. Smartspanner plans start from £15 per user per month, excluding applicable taxes.

How long does it take to implement CMMS?

A focused setup session can create the first site, add initial assets and schedule the first work orders. The time needed for a wider rollout depends on data preparation, integrations, the number of sites and the workflows being introduced.

Does CMMS work on mobile devices?

Yes. Smartspanner provides connected native apps for iOS and Android. Technicians can access and update work orders and scan QR or barcodes from supported mobile devices. The apps require an internet connection and do not operate offline.

What is the difference between CMMS and EAM?

CMMS focuses on maintenance execution and its records, including work orders, preventive maintenance, assets and parts. EAM usually extends further into organisation-wide asset lifecycle, financial and governance processes. The boundaries vary between products, so buyers should compare the workflows and controls they actually require.
