Routine Checks Should Not Disappear Into Paper—or Bury Maintenance Teams in Work Orders

Pre-use equipment checks are different from scheduled maintenance. They are short, operator-led checks performed when equipment is about to be used, at the beginning of a shift or as part of a daily walkaround.

Putting every routine check into the work-order queue makes genuine maintenance demand harder to see. Keeping checks in a separate inspection app creates a different problem: defects and evidence sit outside the CMMS that manages the asset and corrective work.

Smartspanner gives equipment checks their own workflow while keeping the resulting history, alerts and work orders connected to maintenance.

Check first. Create work only when work is needed.

A passed check becomes evidence. A failed question becomes an alert or assigned work. The maintenance queue contains the exceptions—not hundreds of routine confirmations.

Equipment Checks and UK Regulatory Guidance

Digital records support a well-designed inspection system, but the required check, frequency and competence still depend on the equipment and risk.

PUWER: Risk-Based Work Equipment Inspection

HSE says work equipment should be inspected in line with risk assessment. In many cases a quick visual check before use is sufficient; where a formal inspection is required, its result should be recorded and kept at least until the next inspection.

HSE also warns against collecting unnecessary detail that turns checks into superficial tick-box activity. Smartspanner routines should therefore be tailored to the equipment and what is necessary for safety.

Read HSE guidance on inspection of work equipment

Lift Trucks: Documented Pre-Shift Checks

HSE's rider-operated lift-truck guidance calls for a documented pre-shift check, a defect-reporting and remedial-work system, planned maintenance and periodic thorough examination. The operator should check the truck at the beginning of each shift and report safety defects.

An asset-specific routine can make that check consistent and route negative responses into maintenance follow-up.

Read HSE lift-truck guidance (L117)

LOLER: Checks Do Not Replace Thorough Examination

HSE notes that users may need simple pre-use checks for lifting chains and slings or daily checks for lift trucks. These routine checks complement maintenance and statutory thorough examination—they do not replace examination by a competent person where LOLER requires it.

Read HSE lifting-equipment guidance

DVSA: Daily Walkarounds and Defect Records

Commercial vehicle operators must have meaningful daily walkaround checks and a process for reporting defects or nil defects. DVSA's roadworthiness guidance says driver defect reports and rectification records must be retained for at least 15 months.

Read the DVSA roadworthiness guide

Start With One High-Value Routine

A useful equipment-check programme does not begin by digitising every paper form. Start with a check that is repeated frequently, has a clear owner and produces meaningful action when something fails.

  • Choose one asset class, such as lift trucks or mobile plant.
  • Build questions from the risk assessment, manufacturer instructions and competent operational knowledge.
  • Define the negative responses and whether each should alert or create work.
  • Pilot the routine with operators and remove unnecessary questions.
  • Review whether defects are being closed, not just whether forms are being completed.

Equipment Check Software FAQs

What is equipment check software?

Equipment check software replaces paper pre-use forms and walkaround sheets with structured digital routines. Operators identify the equipment, answer the required questions, add evidence and submit a timestamped result. Problems can then be escalated for maintenance action.

Can an operator start a check by scanning a QR code?

Yes. Smartspanner users can scan an asset QR code from the equipment-check workflow. The system identifies the asset and presents the active check routines assigned to it.

How is an equipment check different from a work order?

An equipment check is an on-demand operator inspection used to confirm condition before use or at the start of a shift. A work order records maintenance work that needs planning, assignment or completion. Smartspanner keeps routine checks separate and creates work orders only when a configured response requires follow-up.

What happens when an equipment check fails?

Negative responses such as No, Fail, At risk or Poor mark the check as Action Required. Depending on the checklist configuration, the response raises a dashboard alert or creates a follow-up work order.

Does equipment check software make us PUWER compliant?

No software can guarantee compliance. Smartspanner helps organisations implement and evidence the inspection process they have defined. The organisation remains responsible for its risk assessments, inspection scope and frequency, competent people, safe systems of work and decisions about whether equipment is safe to use.

Do pre-use checks replace LOLER thorough examinations?

No. Routine operator checks and statutory thorough examinations have different purposes. Pre-use checks may help identify visible defects between examinations, but they do not replace a thorough examination by a competent person where LOLER requires one.
