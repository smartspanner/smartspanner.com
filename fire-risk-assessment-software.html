What Is a Fire Risk Assessment?

A fire risk assessment (FRA) is a structured review of a premises to identify what could cause a fire, who could be harmed, and what controls are needed to reduce the risk. Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, every Responsible Person must carry one out, record significant findings if their organisation has five or more employees, and review the assessment whenever something material changes — and at least annually as good practice.

The Government's published guidance and the British Standard PAS 79-1 / 79-2 frame an FRA around five steps:

  1. Identify fire hazards — sources of ignition, fuel, and oxygen
  2. Identify people at risk — employees, visitors, sleeping occupants, lone workers, anyone who'd need help to evacuate
  3. Evaluate the risk and decide whether existing controls are adequate
  4. Record findings, prepare an emergency plan, and provide training
  5. Review and revise the assessment regularly
