What Is a Fire Risk Assessment?

A fire risk assessment (FRA) is a structured review of premises to identify what could cause a fire, who could be harmed and what controls are needed to reduce the risk. The Responsible Person must keep a written record of the full assessment and fire-safety arrangements.

The assessment must be reviewed regularly, particularly when there is reason to suspect it is no longer valid or following a significant relevant change. The Fire Safety Order does not set a universal review interval. Government guidance recognises an annual review as good practice for small and simple premises, while the appropriate frequency depends on the circumstances, complexity and risk.

Government guidance describes five steps:

  1. Identify fire hazards — sources of ignition, fuel, and oxygen
  2. Identify people at risk — employees, visitors, sleeping occupants, lone workers, anyone who'd need help to evacuate
  3. Evaluate the risk and decide whether existing controls are adequate
  4. Record findings, prepare an emergency plan, and provide training
  5. Review and revise the assessment regularly
