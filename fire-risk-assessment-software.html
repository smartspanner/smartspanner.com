What Is a Fire Risk Assessment?

A fire risk assessment (FRA) is a structured review of a premises to identify what could cause a fire, who could be harmed, and what controls are needed to reduce the risk. Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, every Responsible Person must carry one out, record significant findings if their organisation has five or more employees, and review the assessment whenever something material changes — and at least annually as good practice.

The Government's published guidance and the British Standard PAS 79-1 / 79-2 frame an FRA around five steps:

  1. Identify fire hazards — sources of ignition, fuel, and oxygen
  2. Identify people at risk — employees, visitors, sleeping occupants, lone workers, anyone who'd need help to evacuate
  3. Evaluate the risk and decide whether existing controls are adequate
  4. Record findings, prepare an emergency plan, and provide training
  5. Review and revise the assessment regularly

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is legally responsible for a fire risk assessment in the UK?

Under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, the duty falls on the "Responsible Person" — usually the employer for workplaces, the occupier for non-employer premises, or the person with control over the building (often the freeholder, managing agent, or facilities manager) for the common parts of residential blocks. The Fire Safety Act 2021 confirms that external walls and flat entrance doors of multi-occupied residential buildings are in scope.

How often does a fire risk assessment need to be reviewed?

The law requires the assessment to be kept up to date and reviewed if there's reason to believe it's no longer valid — for example, after a layout change, new equipment, change of use, an incident, or significant staff change. Industry good practice, and most insurers, expect a full review at least annually. SmartSpanner sets a 12-month expiry on every assessment by default and emails the owner before the deadline.

Can SmartSpanner be used by a competent fire risk assessor (e.g. PAS 79 / IFE)?

Yes. The structure mirrors UK guidance and PAS 79 methodology. External assessors can be added as users, complete assessments on the platform, and sign off — or be recorded by name only as the responsible assessor while a SmartSpanner user signs off the record.

What happens to the previous year's assessment when we renew?

It's archived. SmartSpanner enforces one active assessment per site, but all historical assessments remain accessible under a History tab on that site, locked and timestamped. The clone-for-renewal workflow carries the previous content into a fresh draft, so the assessor focuses on what's changed.

Can I produce a PDF for my insurer or fire officer?

Yes. Every assessment produces a structured, branded PDF on demand. You can download it locally or email it directly from inside SmartSpanner to one or more recipients, with a cover note. Both downloads and email sends are logged for audit.

Does SmartSpanner replace a fire risk assessor?

No. The platform is a tool for the Responsible Person and any assessor they appoint. Where a competent external assessor is required (high-risk premises, sleeping risk, complex buildings), SmartSpanner is the place where that work is captured, signed off, and renewed — but the competence of the assessor remains the responsibility of the duty holder.
