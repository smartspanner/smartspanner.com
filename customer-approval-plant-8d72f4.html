AMES Goldsmith UK operates two distinct manufacturing environments. Liverpool carries out large-scale chemical and metals conversion at ton scale, while its Dundee sister plant has a stronger R&D focus and operates at kilogram scale.

Across the two sites, approximately 35–40 employees work with an equipment base that includes machinery more than 30 years old. Equipment reliability, maintenance history and parts obsolescence therefore matter alongside the immediate task of keeping production moving.

Managing maintenance across multiple spreadsheets

AMES already had a maintenance process, but it required significant manual administration. Different elements of the maintenance record were held in several Excel workbooks. Jobs were listed and marked as complete by hand, while supporting certificates were retained separately on paper.

An earlier Access database had also been used, but it had become corrupted before Mike Gray joined the company. In practice, the spreadsheets had become the working maintenance system.

Planning and assigning jobs took time. Work in progress was difficult to follow, statutory inspection dates depended on somebody opening the correct file, and engineering KPI data had to be assembled before it could reveal where maintenance problems were occurring.

The functionality AMES needed, without unnecessary complexity

AMES assessed several CMMS options. The objective was not to acquire the largest possible system; it was to put a proper maintenance process in place with the equipment register, job control, preventive maintenance, due dates, history, mobile working, document storage and reporting the operation required.

Mike had previously worked with enterprise-scale CMMS platforms at Amazon and Toyota. That experience gave him a useful point of comparison when assessing how much functionality AMES needed and how much system complexity the team would have to carry in everyday use.

Smartspanner provided the operational depth AMES required while allowing the team to begin with the core workflow and introduce further controls progressively.

Start with jobs. Build from there.

AMES did not attempt to implement every process at once. The team first concentrated on logging maintenance jobs, then added the information and controls that made the workload more useful.

Jobs Times and due dates Engineer utilisation KPI reporting PM and inspections

After approximately 18 months of full rollout, AMES had logged more than 1,100 jobs. Over 20 preventive maintenance activities now recur automatically on time-based schedules, and Mike continues to add more tasks each week.

The implementation is progressively moving AMES from maintenance dominated by reactive demand towards a more structured preventive maintenance operation, without requiring the whole system to be designed before the first job could be managed.

Maintenance information where the work happens

Operators use tablets to log maintenance jobs from the production environment. The issue enters the same workload used by the maintenance team, removing the delay and repeated entry involved in returning to a spreadsheet.

Before

Issue occurs → remember it → update a spreadsheet → assign the work

Now

Issue occurs → operator logs the job → maintenance sees it → work is assigned and tracked

The mobile workflow has been well received by the team. Outstanding jobs and their priorities are available without requiring somebody to reconcile separate files first.

Everyone can see what needs attention

Smartspanner gives AMES a current view of active, due and overdue jobs alongside priorities, preventive maintenance, contractor work and statutory inspections.

Saving an estimated 8–10 hours every week

Customer estimate

8–10 hours of administration saved each week

Mike's estimate covers time previously spent scheduling work, collating engineering reports and KPI data, tracking outstanding jobs and working out which areas of the plant required more attention.

The benefit also reaches the maintenance team. Engineers can immediately see outstanding work and priorities, helping them concentrate on the highest-priority activities first.

From last-minute inspection reminders to planned preparation

Pressure vessel examinations provide a specific example of how the process has changed. The dates were previously recorded in Excel, so the system depended on Mike opening the spreadsheet and noticing that an examination was approaching.

An insurance-company reminder could then leave AMES preparing equipment at short notice. The due dates are now recorded in Smartspanner, with the reminder lead time chosen by the user.

Maintenance and statutory requirements in one place

AMES is bringing a broad range of statutory and compliance activity into the same maintenance record. Smartspanner helps the team organise schedules, reminders, documents and evidence; AMES remains responsible for defining and meeting its applicable legal and regulatory duties.

From recording maintenance to understanding maintenance

Under the spreadsheet process, turning maintenance activity into useful management information required more manual work. Smartspanner now makes it faster to collate engineering and KPI data and identify areas of the plant that need more attention.

Mike uses that information for engineering reports and KPI reporting to AMES' US parent company. The data recorded through everyday maintenance can therefore inform management without creating a separate reporting record.

What's next for the AMES maintenance system?

The implementation is continuing to develop. AMES is considering three further workflows; these are future steps rather than completed parts of the rollout.

Digital permits to work

Exploring digital hot-work, confined-space, excavation and other high-risk permits currently managed on paper.

QR asset identification

Considering durable labels suitable for the corrosive environment so operators can scan directly to an asset record.

Lockout / tagout

Exploring numbered physical locks linked to maintenance jobs as a development from the current cardboard tags.

From spreadsheets to a system built around the operation

AMES has progressively moved from a manual, spreadsheet-based process towards a central digital maintenance system. Reactive work, preventive maintenance, contractor activity and statutory inspections are visible in one place, while completed work provides useful information for engineering and management reporting.

The biggest change is not simply the introduction of new software. AMES now has a maintenance process that is less dependent on separate spreadsheets, repeated administration or one person remembering what needs to happen next.
