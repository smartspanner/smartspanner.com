Smartspanner guide

From spreadsheets to CMMS in 30 days

A practical rollout plan for maintenance teams that need to move from spreadsheet tracking to a structured maintenance management system without disrupting day-to-day work.

Use this guide when:
  • Work orders are tracked in spreadsheets, shared files, or paper forms.
  • Preventive maintenance relies on someone remembering to check a schedule.
  • Managers struggle to see open jobs, overdue work, asset history, and costs.
  • The team wants a realistic CMMS rollout, not a long consulting project.

Before you start

Do not try to rebuild every spreadsheet on day one. The goal is to move the core maintenance workflow first: assets, jobs, preventive maintenance, parts, and reporting. Keep the first rollout narrow enough that technicians can use it in real work within the first month.

The 30-day plan

Days 1-7: build your asset foundation

  • List the sites, areas, systems, and critical assets you want in the first rollout.
  • Clean your asset register enough to import it, but avoid perfection delays.
  • Decide which asset fields matter now: name, location, type, serial number, supplier, and service interval.
  • Assign an owner for asset data quality during the rollout.

Days 8-14: replace spreadsheet job tracking

  • Create standard work order categories, priorities, and completion statuses.
  • Move open jobs from spreadsheets into the CMMS.
  • Agree what technicians must record before closing a job: notes, labour, parts, photos, and downtime where relevant.
  • Start using mobile updates for live job progress instead of end-of-shift spreadsheet edits.

Days 15-21: introduce preventive maintenance

  • Pick the highest-value recurring tasks first, especially safety, compliance, and production-critical checks.
  • Set frequencies and owners for each schedule.
  • Attach procedures, checklists, and photos where they reduce ambiguity.
  • Review the first generated PM jobs and adjust wording before expanding the list.

Days 22-30: add reporting and improve the routine

  • Track open jobs, overdue work, completed PMs, downtime, and recurring asset issues.
  • Review the dashboard with maintenance leads at least once per week.
  • Identify the spreadsheet reports that can now be retired.
  • Choose the next rollout area: spare parts, purchasing, QR codes, procedures, IoT monitoring, or multi-site reporting.

Common pitfalls

  • Trying to migrate everything: move the data that supports daily maintenance decisions first.
  • Designing for managers only: technicians must be able to update jobs quickly from the floor.
  • Keeping two systems alive: once a workflow is live in the CMMS, stop updating the old spreadsheet.
  • Skipping ownership: every asset group, PM schedule, and open-job list needs a named owner.
  • Waiting for perfect data: clean enough to start is better than delaying the rollout for months.

30-day checklist

  • Asset hierarchy agreed
  • First asset register imported
  • Work order categories and priorities configured
  • Open spreadsheet jobs moved into the CMMS
  • Technicians shown how to update jobs on mobile
  • First preventive maintenance schedules live
  • Procedures or checklists attached to critical PMs
  • Dashboard reviewed with the maintenance lead
  • Old spreadsheet reports marked for retirement
  • Next rollout phase agreed

What success looks like

By the end of the first month, your team should know where work is logged, who owns each job, which preventive tasks are due, and which assets are creating repeat problems. That is the point where the CMMS becomes the maintenance system of record and spreadsheets can move back to analysis, not day-to-day control.
