Audit-Ready Maintenance Checklist for Manufacturers

Use this checklist to prepare the records an inspector, insurer, auditor, or customer may ask for. It is a practical readiness tool, not legal advice.

Self-test: if someone asked for any of these records right now, how long would it take to produce them?

1. PUWER - Work Equipment and Machine Guarding

  • Asset list covering production machinery, tools, and work equipment.
  • Inspection and maintenance records for machine guards, interlocks, stops, and safety devices.
  • Evidence that defects were raised, assigned, and closed.
  • Records tied to the asset, not only to a paper form or spreadsheet row.

2. LOLER - Lifting Equipment

  • Register of lifting equipment and accessories.
  • Examination intervals and next due dates.
  • Thorough examination reports and related maintenance actions.
  • Evidence that failed or restricted equipment was taken out of use and followed up.

3. PSSR - Pressure Systems

  • Register of pressure vessels, compressors, boilers, and relevant systems.
  • Written scheme of examination details and inspection schedule.
  • Maintenance records, corrective actions, and evidence of completed remedial work.
  • Clear ownership for upcoming examinations and overdue actions.

4. COSHH and LEV

  • Records for hazardous substances and related controls.
  • Local exhaust ventilation inspection and maintenance records where applicable.
  • Evidence of routine checks, filter changes, defects, and follow-up work.
  • Accessible records for the relevant line, area, or asset.

5. Electrical Safety

  • EICR or fixed wire testing records and next due dates.
  • Portable appliance or equipment inspection records where used.
  • Corrective actions from electrical inspections tracked through to closure.
  • Supporting evidence linked to the site, asset, or work order.

6. Fire Risk Assessments

  • Current fire risk assessment for each relevant site or premises.
  • Review dates, renewal reminders, and responsible owner.
  • Findings, photos, actions, and sign-off evidence retained together.
  • Open actions tracked until completed, not left inside the assessment document.

7. Permits to Work

  • Permit records for hot work, confined spaces, isolation, work at height, and other controlled jobs.
  • Authorisation, acceptance, signatures, users, dates, and supporting files retained.
  • Permits linked to the related asset, site, work order, or contractor activity.
  • Closed permits archived for audit, insurance, or incident review.

8. Routine Checklists and Inspections

  • Inspection templates for daily, weekly, monthly, and annual checks.
  • Timestamped completion records showing who completed each check.
  • Photos, readings, pass/fail answers, notes, and failed-item follow-up.
  • Reports that show completed, missed, failed, and overdue inspections.

9. What You Should Be Able to Produce Quickly

  • The last completed service or inspection for a named machine.
  • A list of overdue safety or compliance tasks.
  • Proof that a failed inspection created a corrective action.
  • Evidence that corrective action was completed.
  • A report for an inspector, insurer, auditor, or customer without rebuilding it from paper, email, and spreadsheets.
If producing the record takes longer than doing the work did, the maintenance system is the weak point.
