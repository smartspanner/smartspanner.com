Audit-Ready Maintenance Checklist

Use this checklist to prepare the maintenance records an inspector, insurer, auditor, or customer may ask for. It is not legal advice, but it gives your team a practical structure for proving maintenance happened.

The goal: show what work was required, when it was due, who completed it, what evidence was captured, and what happened when something failed.

1. Asset and Site Records

  • Current asset list with names, locations, serial numbers, and responsible teams.
  • Site or area records for facilities, production lines, or multi-site operations.
  • Critical assets clearly marked so high-risk equipment can be prioritised.
  • Linked files such as manuals, certificates, drawings, and service instructions.

2. Planned Maintenance Schedule

  • Recurring maintenance tasks with clear frequencies and due dates.
  • Safety, compliance, and production-critical tasks separated from routine work.
  • Assigned owners or teams for each recurring maintenance requirement.
  • Evidence that overdue tasks are visible and actively managed.

3. Completed Work Evidence

  • Completed work orders with date, time, asset, technician, and completion notes.
  • Photos, files, readings, or comments attached where they support the record.
  • Clear distinction between completed, cancelled, deferred, and overdue work.
  • History retained against the asset, not only in a separate spreadsheet or inbox.

4. Checklists and Inspections

  • Inspection templates for routine checks, safety checks, and asset-specific checks.
  • Timestamped completion records for each checklist run.
  • Pass/fail answers, readings, notes, and photos captured where needed.
  • Failed inspection items converted into assigned follow-up work.

5. Fire Risk Assessment Records

  • Latest fire risk assessment for each relevant site or premises.
  • Renewal dates and reminders visible before assessments expire.
  • Observations, photos, actions, and sign-off details retained together.
  • Open findings tracked through to completion as work orders or actions.

6. Permit-to-Work Records

  • Permit records for higher-risk work such as hot work, confined space, isolation, or work at height.
  • Authorisation, acceptance, signatures, dates, users, and supporting files retained.
  • Permits linked to the related work order, asset, site, or contractor activity.
  • Closed permits archived so they can be found during audit or incident review.

7. Reporting Pack

  • Completed maintenance report for the requested date range.
  • Overdue maintenance report showing what is outstanding and who owns it.
  • Inspection completion and failed-item reports.
  • Evidence exports or PDFs ready to share with the auditor, insurer, customer, or internal reviewer.

8. Fast Readiness Check

  • Can you find the last completed service for a named asset in under two minutes?
  • Can you show what scheduled work is overdue today?
  • Can you prove who completed a safety inspection and when?
  • Can you show what happened after an inspection failed?
  • Can you export a report without rebuilding it from paper, email, or spreadsheets?
If any answer is "not quickly", the issue is usually not the work itself. It is the recordkeeping system around the work.
