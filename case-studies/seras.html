When Seras expanded through acquisition, it inherited more than new equipment and facilities. It also inherited different maintenance management systems.

Already a Smartspanner customer for several years, Seras faced a choice: continue using different systems across its sites, or bring maintenance together on one platform.

The decision was to expand Smartspanner across the growing business.

Keeping biomass processing equipment running

Seras processes waste wood into biomass fuel for the renewable energy sector, operating across seven sites in the UK.

Its operations rely on heavy-duty equipment, including shredders, screens, loading shovels, material handlers and specialist processing lines.

Equipment reliability is critical. Interruptions to production can affect fuel deliveries to power stations, making planned maintenance, accurate records and effective repair management essential.

From spreadsheets to structured maintenance

Seras originally introduced Smartspanner to manage planned preventive maintenance on a specialist processing line at Tilbury.

Previously, maintenance checks were managed through Excel spreadsheets, with paper records and contractor job cards stored separately.

The business needed a more reliable way to record inspections, servicing and repairs, particularly for equipment with long-term contractual maintenance requirements.

After evaluating several systems, Seras selected Smartspanner.

Ease of setup, straightforward operation and the ability to control user access were important factors in that decision.

As the business developed, so did its maintenance requirements. Seras established an in-house engineering team and began extending its use of Smartspanner beyond planned maintenance to include equipment checks, reactive work orders and maintenance costs.

One system for a growing business

Following the acquisition of additional sites, Seras inherited maintenance systems including Agility and iAuditor.

This presented an opportunity to review how maintenance would be managed across the enlarged business.

Rather than maintain several separate platforms, Seras wanted to standardise its approach.

Tony Carnall, who oversees the system, demonstrated Smartspanner to the wider team, including company directors.

The decision to expand Smartspanner was straightforward.

The rollout is now progressing across the additional sites, with existing asset records imported into Smartspanner and local teams receiving training.

Local control. Complete visibility.

Managing maintenance across multiple sites creates a challenge: each location needs access to its own equipment and work, while management needs visibility across the wider operation.

For Seras, this was a key reason for choosing Smartspanner.

Individual sites can focus on their own assets and work orders, reducing confusion and keeping day-to-day maintenance relevant to the people carrying it out.

At the same time, Tony can filter maintenance information by location or review activity across the business.

This is particularly valuable for Seras's engineering team, which supports sites across different regions of the UK.

Work can be assigned to the appropriate engineers, with each site maintaining control of its own maintenance activities.

From maintenance records to better decisions

Smartspanner is also helping Seras bring maintenance information together.

Previously, retrieving the history of an asset could mean searching through spreadsheets, paper records, contractor documentation and emails.

Now, work orders can hold the relevant job information, supporting documents and repair history against the asset itself.

For a business supplying fuel to power stations, that traceability matters.

If equipment fails or a delivery is disrupted, Seras needs to understand what maintenance has been completed, what repairs were required and why an asset was unavailable.

Smartspanner also provides a breakdown of work order costs, including labour, spare parts and external contractors.

This gives managers a clearer picture of the cost of repairs and helps identify where maintenance resources are being used.

As the wider rollout progresses, Seras plans to make greater use of this information for maintenance performance reporting and operational decisions.

Software that grows with the business

One of the reasons Seras has continued with Smartspanner is the support behind the software.

As the business has grown and its requirements have changed, the Smartspanner team has worked with Seras to accommodate new needs or identify practical solutions.

For Tony, that responsiveness has been an important part of the relationship.

Combined with a system that is straightforward for individual sites to use and flexible enough to support a growing operation, it has given Seras the confidence to extend Smartspanner across the business.

Building a consistent approach to maintenance

Seras is continuing to bring its sites and engineering teams together on Smartspanner.

What began as a way to manage preventive maintenance on one processing line is developing into a common platform for maintenance planning, equipment checks, work orders, asset history and cost visibility across the business.

For a growing multi-site operation, the objective is simple: consistent maintenance processes, reliable information and less time spent managing disconnected systems.

One system. Seven sites. A clearer view of maintenance.
